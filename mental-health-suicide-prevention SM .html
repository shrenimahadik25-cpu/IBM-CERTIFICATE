Understanding
What is Mental Health?

Mental health includes emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how we think, feel, and act — at every stage of life.

😞

Depression

Persistent sadness, loss of interest, low energy, and feelings of hopelessness that last weeks or longer.

😰

Anxiety Disorders

Excessive worry or fear that interferes with daily activities; includes panic disorder, social anxiety, and PTSD.

🌊

Bipolar Disorder

Extreme mood swings between emotional highs (mania) and lows (depression).

🧩

Schizophrenia

A serious disorder affecting how a person thinks, feels, and behaves; may include hallucinations or delusions.

🍽️

Eating Disorders

Conditions like anorexia, bulimia, and binge-eating disorder involving unhealthy eating behaviors and body image.

💔

PTSD

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder develops after experiencing or witnessing traumatic events, causing flashbacks and distress.

✗ Myth

"Mental illness is a sign of weakness."

✓ Fact

Mental illness is a medical condition, just like diabetes or heart disease. It has nothing to do with strength.

✗ Myth

"Talking about suicide plants the idea."

✓ Fact

Asking about suicide openly does NOT increase risk. It can provide relief and open the door to getting help.

✗ Myth

"People who talk about suicide just want attention."

✓ Fact

Any talk of suicide should be taken seriously. Most people who die by suicide gave warning signs beforehand.

✗ Myth

"You can't help someone who is suicidal."

✓ Fact

Your presence, compassion, and listening matter enormously. Connecting someone to help can save a life.

🌍

1 in 4 People

Will be affected by mental health issues at some point in their life globally.

📊

700,000+ Per Year

People die by suicide every year worldwide. It is the 4th leading cause of death among 15–29 year-olds.

⏳

11 Years Average

Delay between symptom onset and treatment for mental health conditions.

💚

80% Treatable

With proper care and support, about 80% of people with mental illness can significantly improve their quality of life.

Awareness
Warning Signs of Suicidal Crisis

Know the signs. Early recognition can save a life. If you notice these in yourself or others, seek help immediately.

  • Talking about wanting to die or to kill oneself
  • Looking for ways to kill oneself (searching online, collecting pills)
  • Talking about being a burden to others
  • Increased substance use (alcohol, drugs)
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, and activities
  • Extreme mood swings or sudden calmness after depression
  • Giving away prized possessions
  • Expressing feelings of hopelessness or having no reason to live
  • Talking about being trapped or in unbearable pain
  • Saying goodbyes or acting as if settling affairs
  • Reckless or self-destructive behavior
  • Changes in sleep patterns — sleeping too much or too little
Self-Help
Coping Strategies That Work

Evidence-based techniques to manage stress, anxiety, and difficult emotions day to day.

Grounding (5-4-3-2-1)

  • 5 things you can see
  • 4 things you can touch
  • 3 things you can hear
  • 2 things you can smell
  • 1 thing you can taste

Mindfulness

  • Focus on the present moment
  • Observe thoughts without judgment
  • Body scan meditation
  • Mindful eating or walking

Physical Activity

  • 30-min walk outdoors
  • Yoga or stretching
  • Dance to music you love
  • Even 10 mins helps mood

Journaling

  • Write 3 things you're grateful for
  • Express emotions freely
  • Identify triggers and patterns
  • Write a letter to yourself

Social Connection

  • Call a trusted friend or family
  • Join a support group
  • Volunteer in community
  • Share your feelings openly

Professional Help

  • Therapy (CBT, DBT, talk)
  • Psychiatry if needed
  • Online counseling platforms
  • Ask your GP for a referral

Supporting Others
How to Help Someone in Crisis

You don't need to be a professional to make a difference. Here's what you can do.

👂

Listen Without Judgment

Give them your full attention. Don't minimize their feelings or offer quick fixes — just listen.

💬

Ask Directly

It's okay to ask "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking shows you care and opens the door.

🤝

Stay With Them

Don't leave them alone. Your presence provides comfort and reduces immediate risk.

📞

Connect to Help

Help them call a crisis line, go to the ER, or contact a mental health professional together.

🚫

Remove Access

If safe to do so, help remove access to lethal means such as medications, weapons, or sharp objects.

💙

Follow Up

Check in after the crisis. A simple message or call says "I care about you" and can prevent relapse.
